## The truth in brief

Hector Navarro, 39, a cargo checker at East Harbor Terminal, noticed in November that export containers he had checked in were leaving the terminal in the middle of the night through the contractor gate, coming back before dawn heavier, and wearing different seals. He photographed seals, kept a notebook, watched the gate one night, and on Thursday, November 29 put it in writing to his supervisor, Russell Gaffney.

Gaffney had been approving those night moves for a year. Gideon Kane, whose company Kane Logistics runs drayage and security at the terminal, had made him: in October 2017 Kane showed him a Meridian Advisory Group intelligence file on the repayment deal Gaffney signed in Norfolk in 2012 and has lied about on every disclosure form since. Gaffney dismissed Hector in writing on Friday morning and texted Kane that evening. Kane said, "Leave him to me." On Saturday Kane asked Gaffney what shift Hector worked Sunday, then told Dwayne Fisk, his security subcontractor, to get Hector's notebook and phone and "make him understand. Nothing that brings police."

On Sunday, December 2, Hector finished his shift, locked his notebook in his gear locker as he had every night since November 20, and went into the restricted staging area to photograph one container's seal before it sailed. Fisk drove in the contractor gate at 6:12 PM, badged into the staging area at 6:21, and found him there. Hector refused to hand anything over. In the struggle Fisk struck him with a steel binder bar from the lashing gear cage. Hector died. Fisk took his phone, emptied his wallet and turned out his pockets to look like a robbery, dragged him behind a stack of flat racks, wiped the bar and hung it back on the rack, and drove out the contractor gate at 7:03. He never found the notebook.

Kane paid Fisk $12,000 the next morning through Patapsco Freight Services, a shell company Adrian Holt set up for Meridian's money, told him to say he had been on the Riverside yard gate all night, and told Gaffney to "clean up the paper." Kane did not order the killing. He ordered the intimidation and the theft, and then hid the man who killed.

Kane is not at the top. His own insurance file, in the safe at his Riverside yard office, holds an unsigned memorandum telling him that "no direct contact with the Architect is permitted without authorization."

**Solution.** Dwayne Fisk killed Hector Navarro with a steel binder bar from the lashing gear cage at Staging Area C, while trying to take his notebook and phone. Gideon Kane sent him to intimidate Hector and take his records, did not order the killing, and paid Fisk and coached his alibi afterward. Hector was targeted because he was documenting the night moves and seal changes. Russell Gaffney approved and covered for the moves under coercion from a Meridian intelligence file. Meridian money (through a shell Holt organized) and Meridian intelligence (Rourke's file on Gaffney) kept the freight operation running. Above them all is someone the records call the Architect, who is not identified.

**Author only, never in play or in the closing:** the Kane contractor agreement (E022) that arrives by courier on Tuesday was pulled from Meridian's archive on Monday, December 3 and sent through the Clearwater channel on Evelyn Lockhart's instruction (bible section 4). Nobody in this case comments on its footer or its waybill.

## The mechanism

**The freight.** Stolen commercial goods (generators, power tools, survey instruments, electronics from mid-Atlantic warehouse and truck thefts) and bulk cash leave the country inside legitimate export containers. A container is booked by Harborside Export Trading with a harmless cargo ("used tires," "household effects"), trucked to East Harbor, checked in at Gate 1 by a checker with its seal and weight recorded, and staged in Staging Area C. Then, on a weeknight, a Kane Logistics drayage move takes it out Gate 4 to the Riverside yard, where the cargo is swapped or topped up behind closed bay doors and a new seal applied. It comes back before dawn. Gaffney approves each move by phone as "special handling" under Amendment No. 2, with no entry in the daily move plan. Afterward the dock receipt is "corrected" to the new seal and weight, at first by Joanne Lindqvist on Gaffney's instruction, later by Gaffney himself.

Fourteen containers since September 10, 2018; eight of them (Oct 22 onward) can be reconstructed from paper (E036).
