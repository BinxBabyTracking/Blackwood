## The truth in brief

Claire Donovan, 34, spent six years as a records analyst at Meridian Advisory Group's Records Center in Chantilly, Virginia. In September 2018 Miriam Castell, program director of the Clearwater Accountability Initiative (a Washington transparency nonprofit funded, openly, by the Lockhart Foundation), approached her. Castell had been told by an anonymous "senior source inside Meridian," through unsigned typed notes, that Claire had the access and the conscience to help. Claire agreed.

Three times Castell passed on what "the investigators need next." Each time Claire pulled one box, copied one item on the Records Center copier, and handed the copy to Castell, who sent it by Capital Courier on Clearwater's account 20417 with the sender left blank: the Tri-County invoice to the Lockhart Foundation in New York (Oct 29), the intelligence client schedule to Blackwood in Pittsburgh (Nov 20), and the Kane contractor agreement to Blackwood (Dec 4). Every page was genuine. Each one named the man at the bottom of the chain. The item filed with each one, the Executive Office cover sheet or routing slip that showed who stood above him, was never asked for. After Martin Keller died, Claire began copying those for herself.

Holt, Rourke and Kane were arrested in that order, the order of a compliance memo Claire's mentor Alan Corbett had sent to Meridian's Executive Office in July. Claire saw the pattern, resigned on Friday, December 7, put a carton of duplicates and an encrypted drive into private storage in Lorton, and on Tuesday night, December 11, wrote to Blackwood asking to meet at 10:00 the next morning.

She was already being watched, and from two directions. Julian Rourke, in federal detention since November 21, had seen the copier footer on the client list at his detention hearing; Meridian Security's records account traced the box to Claire's badge. Through a "defense investigator," Dean Whitlock, who carried notes in and out of legal visits, Rourke sent Brandon Tate, a security contractor who worked for his division, to find her, take her papers and learn what she had told "the Pittsburgh people." Separately, the source learned from Castell that Claire was going to the meeting. At 6:15 on Wednesday morning a man speaking for the source told Castell that Meridian security knew, that Claire must not go, and that a cottage had been chosen where she could wait while "someone came for the papers Friday." Castell booked it and called Claire. Claire believed her.

Claire went to the café at 8:14, left a note for Blackwood in her own hand ("You're looking at the wrong people"), switched off her phone and drove to Laurel Run Cottage in Delaplane. Tate had followed her from her apartment. He went back to Alexandria and searched her apartment with a cloned fob, then returned to the cottage at 9:02 PM, said "Miriam sent me," and was let in. When Claire tried to leave he restrained her with zip ties. At about 1:00 AM he moved her to a house he had leased in Bristow and held her in a padlocked basement room, questioning her about who had asked her to copy the records and what Blackwood had. She never told him about Lorton.

Blackwood found her through the cottage booking, the lock log, the café camera, Tate's plates and the Bristow lease. Police went in at 6:10 AM on Friday, December 14. Claire was alive. Tate was arrested in the kitchen.

**Solution.** Claire Donovan contacted Blackwood to show that the records behind its earlier leads had been selected. She left home willingly on Wednesday, believing she was going into a protective hiding arrangement relayed by Miriam Castell. When she tried to leave the cottage, Brandon Tate detained her and moved her to Bristow. Julian Rourke directed him from detention, through Dean Whitlock, to recover her records and learn what she had disclosed and who had asked her for it. Claire had copied genuine Meridian records chosen by someone else and passed them through Castell. The earlier cases' conclusions stand: the records were real and Holt, Rourke and Kane did what Blackwood found. But the leads were chosen to expose those three and to stop short of whoever authorized them, an authority above them (the Executive Office reference EO-01, the "A" of the routing slips) working through intermediaries, not yet identified.

Author-only, never in this case, not even in the closing: the source is Dr. Evelyn Lockhart; the man on the 6:15 AM call works for her (C06's Conrad Pike); she did not order the abduction. Evelyn appears here only as Clearwater's patron, the recipient of the first package, and a concerned caller.

## The mechanism: how a page left the archive

1. Castell receives an unsigned typed note by hand delivery (a different bike messenger each time) naming the record the "investigators" need. She destroys each note, as the first one told her to. (C06 holds the source's side of these instructions.)
2. Castell messages Claire on an encrypted messaging app (E025).
3. Claire signs into the stack register for a restricted collection, purpose "retention audit," with Benjamin Sayer's approval (E016); checks the box out by badge (E017); copies the item on RM-C2, which prints the footer and logs the job to her badge (E017); returns the box.
4. Claire hands the copy to Castell the same day or the next, and logs it in her own register (E038).
5. Castell sends it by Capital Courier, account 20417, sender blank (E018).
6. From Nov 19 on, Claire also copies the EO item filed beside each released item and keeps it (E038, E040, E041). She photographs 41 more items from the same boxes with her phone (no copier record), scanned later onto the drive (E037).
