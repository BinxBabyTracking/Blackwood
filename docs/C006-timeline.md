## The truth in five sentences

1. In June 1996 Hollow Creek Steel's general manager, Richard Stroud, deferred a known, urgent repair to the gas monitors and exhaust fan in a gas valve gallery to protect a pending land sale; three men died of carbon monoxide on the night of June 13 to 14.
2. Stroud blamed the crew; maintenance director Glenn Sutter re-dated the inspection log, and borough code officer Len Greer buried the union's complaint and signed off, for a cash fee and a $1 building lot.
3. Emily Lasko, 19, was gathering proof of that cover-up when, on Saturday, August 17, 1996, she met her boyfriend Wade Reeves at the closed East Mill to end their relationship over his secret affair and his copper thefts; he struck her, she fell against a steel bench and died, and he hid her in Tool Crib 4, moved her car to the Brackett bus depot and let the town believe she ran away.
4. Her remains were found on October 12, 2018, while Stroud was closing a riverfront redevelopment deal on the same land; when Glenn Sutter decided to tell Blackwood the truth, Stroud had his longtime security contractor Michael Trent kill him on October 25 and stage a robbery.
5. Trent then abducted Len Greer on October 28 to find and destroy Greer's copies of the 1996 papers; police recovered Greer alive on October 30, and his testimony completes the case against Stroud, while the case against Wade rests on separate, personal evidence.

Emily's murder and the industrial cover-up overlap in time and place. They do not share a motive or a perpetrator. The final report must keep five responsibilities apart: the disaster, the 1996 cover-up, Emily's death and concealment, Glenn's murder, and Len's abduction.

## Places

Hollow Creek, Pennsylvania: a fictional borough of about 6,000 on the Monongahela River, south of Pittsburgh, built around Hollow Creek Steel's Riverside Works.

- **Riverside Works.** The West End was the working plant in 1996 (reheat furnaces, rolling mill, the gas valve gallery where the men died). The East Mill closed in 1991 and stood empty, fenced, reached from Mill Road through the East Gate. The whole works closed in 2003.
- **East Mill Maintenance Building.** Two stories of brick. Ground floor: the shop floor, a steel workbench, and Tool Crib 4, a windowless cage room with a steel door and a hasp. Wade stripped copper and brass here in 1996.
- **Gallery B.** The underground gas valve gallery under Reheat Furnace No. 2, West End. Blast furnace gas and natural gas lines, three carbon monoxide monitor heads, exhaust fan EF-3.
- **Brackett.** A town 14 miles up the river with a Greyhound stop at the Brackett Transit Depot.
- **Valley View Motor Inn.** On Route 51 outside town.
- **Corner Mart.** Gas station and store on Mill Road, half a mile from the East Gate.
- **Laurel Hollow Road.** A dirt road in the ridges 11 miles south. At its end, the Trent family hunting camp, a two-room cabin with a cellar, owned through Laurel Hollow Holdings LLC.
- **Creekside Self Storage.** Route 837, 2 miles from Glenn's house. Gated, keypad entry with a logged code per renter and a management override code.

## The two theories (Chapter 5)

**Theory A, the company.** Emily had photographed the deferral memo and was asking questions. Stroud had a motive, and Trent was his man inside the plant. Her body was hidden on company land. This is what Sarah believes and what the player is invited to believe through Chapter 4.

**Theory B, Wade.** The calendar meeting, the pager and Rebecca's call, Andrew's pickup, the cab from Brackett, the seat position, the scrap sales that stop after August 10, the Denise letters, the hasp welded by someone who welds, and Wade's changing account.

What separates them:

- Trent's 1996 guard log puts him at the West End main gate all night on August 17.
- Stroud's expense report and hotel bill put him in Pittsburgh.
- Nobody in the cover-up knew the East Mill tool crib; Wade had been working in that building all summer.
- Emily's prints of the memo were never taken: Sarah still has them, and Evelyn's carbon copy survived. A killer protecting the cover-up would have taken the papers, not the overnight bag.
- Len, who knows everything about the cover-up, says the only time Stroud mentioned Emily was to say the company "had nothing to fear from a teenager with a camera."

## Forensic and evidentiary limits

These must hold in every line of evidence and dialogue.

- Skeletal remains after 22 years: Brooks can say perimortem depressed fracture of the right parietal, consistent with impact against or by a narrow, straight-edged object; no healing; no other perimortem trauma found. He cannot say whether she fell or was struck, the exact time of death, or who was there.
- Identification is by dental comparison. A sibling DNA comparison with Sarah is under way and will not be back during the case.
- Tool Crib 4 was opened by the teenagers, who broke the weld. The weld is a MIG bead consistent with a portable wire-feed welder. It cannot be matched to one machine.
- Nothing recovered carries Wade's DNA or prints from 1996 that would mean anything: he was her boyfriend and had been in her car.
- The cab log has no passenger name. It shows a fare from the Brackett depot to Mill Road at 11:52 PM.
- Andrew's time is wrong by an hour; his description of the truck is right.
- Glenn's autopsy: blunt force injuries to the head consistent with a bar-like object; defensive injuries to the forearms; time of death consistent with the evening of October 25. It cannot name the weapon's owner.
- The storage gate log records codes, not people. The override code belongs to the facility's security contractor, which is Trent Protective Services; who typed it is shown by the other evidence (the truck, the payment, the camp).
- Len's statement is that of a participant in the cover-up. It must be corroborated by documents (the safe deposit papers, the deed, the bank record) before the report relies on it.

## Contradictions found

Fixed while writing this timeline, before any dialogue:

1. The brief made Sarah, Emily's "younger sister," 43 in 2018, which made her older than Emily. She is now 38 (16 in 1996).
2. The brief made Andrew, a "former classmate," 45. He is now 41, as is Rebecca.
3. The brief called Thomas Greer both a "former municipal official" and a "former plant employee." He is the borough's code enforcement and safety officer: a municipal official who dealt with the plant.
4. The brief called Harold Mercer both "maintenance director" and "retired supervisor." He is the retired maintenance director.
5. The brief places Emily's death and the disaster at "the facility." They are kept apart: the disaster was in the working West End; Emily died in the East Mill, closed since 1991, where Wade worked his copper. The cover-up's people had no reason to know Tool Crib 4.
6. Andrew's sighting must be about 8:40 PM to fit his 8:30 PM clock-out and a ten-minute walk. His memory says "about ten," which is the point of his testimony being imperfect.
7. The 724 area code did not exist until 1998, so 1996 Hollow Creek numbers are 412.
8. Joan Reeves gave Wade an alibi in 1996 ("home when I went to bed," 10:30). She was wrong or covering; she died in 2014. Len's own taking is told by Len (E077), so it is no longer closing-report only.
9. Fixed after an independent review (Oct 27): the teenagers cut the crib's chain as well as breaking the weld; the 1996 incident report no longer cites the log (it was rewritten on June 17); the Unit 22 renter saw Glenn's car as he left; documents produced under court order are dated October 31.
