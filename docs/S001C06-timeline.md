## The truth in brief

Dr. Evelyn Lockhart, 52, founded Meridian Advisory Group in 2007. In 2009 she stepped out of every public role and put the company under a Delaware trust, the Cardinal Point Trust, of which she is settlor and the only person with reserved powers. From then on she ran Meridian's criminal side through a paper system of her own design: every "reserved matter" (a payment routed through intermediaries, a program of coercive intelligence, pressure on a witness, a legal structure meant to hide ownership) had to be requested on a numbered two-part form, the EX-7, and approved in ink by the executive code with authority over it. The highest code, A-1, was hers. Operators knew her only as "the Architect," and her notes to them were typed and signed "A." The Records Administrator, Benjamin Sayer, logged every form and note in the Executive Authorization Register, scanned it, recorded a digest of the scan, and filed the original in a locked cage, Bay C, at the Meridian Records Center in Chantilly. The system made every operator's crimes a matter of record, which is how she controlled them and, in 2018, how she sacrificed them.

In August 2018 she decided Adrian Holt (F-2), Julian Rourke (I-3) and Gideon Kane (L-4) had become liabilities. She did not fabricate anything. Through Miriam Castell at the Clearwater Accountability Initiative, a nonprofit her foundation funds, she had Claire Donovan copy three genuine records and ship them by courier, the first to herself. Blackwood solved real crimes with them. Her own register, the only record pointing upward, never left Bay C. To let Claire's pulls through without question she instructed Benjamin, in writing, to approve them; that instruction is what showed him, in October, whose design the leaks were.

Benjamin had been keeping a private duplicate of the register's scans since 2013, synced to an encrypted drive every Friday, with the digests written by hand in a bound custody ledger. On Friday, December 21 he received, through Elena Marchetti, Evelyn's typed instruction to lift the federal legal hold and destroy the archive. He refused in writing, resigned at 3:55 PM and went home with the drive. On Saturday, December 22 Marchetti was arrested (C05). That afternoon Evelyn learned from Meridian's records system that Benjamin had been exporting the executive volume every Friday for five years. She cut Marchetti out and gave the whole job to her security man, Conrad Pike (S-6), in a signed note: destroy everything, and recover what Benjamin took, "not bargained for."

Pike wiped the executive volume on the server on December 23, sent two contractors, Kurt Sobczak and Travis Dunleavy, to watch Benjamin's house, and on the night of Wednesday, December 26 had a shredding contractor empty the finance, client and logistics collections at Chantilly while he and Sobczak went into Bay C, which burned that night. Benjamin, already planning to hide, heard about the fire at 1:14 AM, wrote to Blackwood at 6:12 AM Thursday with one page of the register, and slipped out of the back of his house at 8:10 AM. Pike's men tracked him to a short-stay apartment in Silver Spring through a pretext call to the friend who booked it, Caroline Oduya; he left an hour before they let themselves in. He went by bus to Frederick and by cab to a tenant house in Fulton County, Pennsylvania, that he had leased in November. Pike's men followed the cab and by dawn on Saturday had him penned in. Blackwood found him first by evidence; the FBI and Pennsylvania State Police brought him out alive, and the drive with him.

The archive, imaged at an independent laboratory, matched Benjamin's handwritten digests, Meridian's own last backup and the burned originals recovered from Bay C. It names A-1 as Evelyn Lockhart, and her signature on the surviving originals matches her signatures on the trust's records. It shows her approving coercive intelligence, intimidation and legal concealment, choosing which records Blackwood received and when, and (with her December 22 note, recovered from her own file before a second shredding) directing the destruction of the evidence. It does not show that she ordered any killing. Paul Reddick killed Martin Keller, Dwayne Fisk killed Hector Navarro and Gabriel Knox killed Nathaniel Ogden; no register entry exists for any of those acts. Every earlier finding stands.

**Solution.** Dr. Evelyn Lockhart is the Architect. She controlled the criminal network operating through Meridian Advisory Group by compartmentalized executive authority recorded in a sealed authorization register and funded through her family trust: Holt ran finance, Rourke intelligence and coercion, Kane logistics and enforcement, Marchetti legal concealment. She exposed Holt, Rourke and Kane because they had become liabilities, by directing the release of genuine, selected records that implicated them while hiding her own role. She did not fabricate the crimes, and the earlier findings stand; the evidence does not show she ordered the murders. It establishes her command over specified criminal operations, her direction of the selective disclosures, and her authorization of the destruction of evidence. Conrad Pike carried out the destruction of the Records Center and the pursuit of Benjamin Sayer on her authority. Benjamin fled voluntarily, and was then hunted.

## The mechanism: the Executive Register

This is the case's "money": the thing every document refers to.

| Piece | What it is |

| Form EX-7 | Numbered two-part carbonless authorization slip. The requester fills and signs it; the set goes sealed, through the Records Administrator, to the approving code, who signs "APPROVED" with code and date. ## Legal and not legal

The brief asks the player to tell these apart. Quinlan explains it when asked; the report and closing keep to it.

| Act | Legal? |
| --- | --- |
| The Lockhart Foundation's grants, the Carroway fund, the foundation's public work | Legal and real |
| Announcing a restructuring, divesting the foundation's positions, closing Meridian's bank accounts (Dec 26 to 27) | Legal in themselves |
| Shredding records on a normal schedule | Legal |
| Shredding or burning records under a federal legal hold, altering the schedule to do it, wiping EXVOL | Obstruction of justice |
| Watching, tracking and penning in a witness to take records from him | Witness tampering and intimidation |
| Approving coercive intelligence, pressure on a witness, structures to hide ownership | Criminal authorization (conspiracy) |
| The three murders | The killers' own acts. Not authorized in any record |

- **How Evelyn learned the archive might exist.** The brief says only that she "learns." Fixed: Pike's IT check of Custodia on Dec 22 found Benjamin's weekly exports, and E059 says so.

- **D22 and concealment.** The brief's D22 needs D17, D18, D19 and D21, but its own final confrontation and report require "her involvement in concealment," which is D20. D20 is added to D22's requirements. Not circular.

## Recovery routes (prior-case records)

The finale's required chain reaches back to records the player may never have found in C01 to C05. Each is offered again in C06, with its original canonical id kept, so every series deduction works unchanged. The case engine holds each recovered record as a C06 evidence entry with a local id R01 to R17 and a `canonical` field pointing at the original (for example canonical `S001-C01-E029`).
