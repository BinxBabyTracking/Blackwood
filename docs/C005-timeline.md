# C005 Timeline: The Abbey of Silent Bells

Canonical timeline for C005. Every file in `src/cases/C005/` must agree with it. Written from the design doc (project doc "C005 Case Design - The Abbey of Silent Bells"), Oct 27, 2026.

The hidden clock is for checking only. No clock hour is ever shown or spoken in the game: people and the journal use the abbey's offices and the days of the week.

## The abbey's day (winter observance)
From Holy Cross Day to Lent the abbey keeps its winter day: one meal after None, and the evening offices in failing light. The clock column is hidden, for checking only. It never appears in the game.

| Compline | bell about 5:35, office about 5:40 to 6:00 | Then the Great Silence until Matins | Great bell, nine strokes then tolling until all are in. Hand bell at the church door only if the great bell fails |

The gate is barred at Compline.

### Contradictions found

- **"Shortly before the body was discovered."** The spec has Peter hear the argument shortly before discovery, but also has the murder before Compline and the discovery after it. Fixed: Peter heard it during Collation, about an hour before the body was found, and says "before Compline."
- **Forcing the door would damage the bar.** Fixed: forcing tore out the bracket; the hook scored the bar's top face, which forcing cannot reach. E04 shows both, separately.
- **Why stage a barred room at all.** It made a seizure believable (a man alone, the door barred from inside), which the abbot wanted, and it hid that anyone had been in.
- **Who else holds a passage key.** Only Anselm, and Simon saw him in the church lighting the stalls from the end of Collation.
- **Peter's lost half hour.** He went for oil at 5:10 and was in the court at 5:50. He sat by Margery's fire eating bread, which he hides because he was told to hurry. Margery's statement explains it.
- **Geoffrey could not have returned.** The roll has him out after None; the gate is barred at Compline; William would have let him in. He was not there.
- **The candlestick.** It holds Edmund's short scriptorium candle, not a tall altar candle like its twin's, which is how it gives itself away.
- **The forgery's date.** "The seventh year of Abbot Walter" is right for 1215 (Walter elected 1209), so the date clause is not a discrepancy; it is a decoy in the comparison.
- **Calendar.** All weekdays above checked against the Julian calendar used in 1327.
